# Adam Sabar Mechbal — CV website

## 1. Project analysis: user profile

**User persona:** Laura Martin, 34, a recruiter at a technology company offering internships to Computer Engineering students. She normally reviews candidates on a laptop, although she may open a portfolio on her phone.

**Goals:** Understand Adam's educational background, programming knowledge, interests and practical experience. She wants to distinguish his interest in cybersecurity and cloud engineering from skills he has already applied.

**Needs:** A clear introduction, recognisable section headings, concise evidence of a completed project, work experience and understandable language levels. A link to his GitHub profile helps her explore his public work.

**Frustrations:** Long, generic descriptions, exaggerated expertise, unclear skill ratings and decorative effects that make a CV difficult to read.

## 2. Project analysis: information architecture

The website presents the CV on a single page. Its content follows a clear order: introduction, About me, Education, Skills, Projects, Work experience and Languages. The header contains the candidate's name and six internal navigation links. These links let visitors jump directly to a section, while the page also supports a complete reading from top to bottom.

The introduction gives the visitor the most useful information first: Adam's full name, his current role as a Computer Engineering student and his interest in cybersecurity and cloud engineering. A short paragraph explains his approach to learning and practical projects. The View my project link leads directly to the Projects section.

About me then explains the personal motivation behind those interests. Education appears next, with the current university degree before the completed baccalaureate. The two entries share a simple vertical line to group them visually. Their written dates and status explain which course is still in progress.

Skills are displayed as five named technology blocks. Projects follows with one real example: an ESP32 ventilation automation system installed at a mosque. Its description explains the purpose, software, connectivity and configuration interface without reproducing the complete technical report. Work experience adds the candidate's previous job as a welder, clearly separated from his technical project.

Languages appear near the bottom in five individual blocks with written levels. The footer provides a GitHub link and a Back to top link. On smaller screens, the introduction stacks vertically and the skill and language blocks wrap into additional rows, preserving the content order.

## 3. Project analysis: visual design

I chose a simple, minimal design to keep the focus on my background and make the CV suitable for a student. I avoided animations, 3D images and complex effects so that decoration would not distract from the information. This also keeps the design manageable with the HTML and CSS techniques studied in class.

I chose a neutral palette of navy (#2F4156), teal (#567C8D), sky blue (#C8D9E6), beige (#F5EFEB) and white (#FFFFFF) to give the page a calm, professional appearance. The light colours keep large background areas subtle, while dark navy text stands out against them. I limited the palette and repeated it throughout the page to make the sections and images feel part of the same design. Teal distinguishes supporting details without introducing another colour.

I chose Roboto because its simple letter shapes suit a digital CV. Using one typeface with regular and bold weights avoids unnecessary variation. Different text sizes establish priorities, making my name, section headings and individual entries easy to distinguish. I justified the longer paragraphs to give them aligned edges, while keeping short labels normally aligned to avoid stretched spaces.

Consistent spacing gives the information room and separates topics without adding decoration. Repeated panels and modest rounded corners make related items look consistent and keep the page approachable. The project panel has a thin teal border, and the introduction links directly to it, because the installed ESP32 system provides practical evidence of my skills.

I chose flat imagery to support my interests without overwhelming the text. Keeping their style and colour consistent avoids a visually crowded result. Written skill names and language levels provide clear information without subjective percentage bars.

Finally, I kept the same content order on smaller screens so that adapting the layout would preserve the intended reading sequence.
